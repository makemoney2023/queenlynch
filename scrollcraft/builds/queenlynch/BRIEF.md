Self-authored, not interviewed.

The human asked to copy skills and then use the scrollcraft scrollworld skill to improve the existing Queen Lynch Pharmacy site. They were not asked the eight questions in conversation. Answers below are written from the salvage pack, the live copy, and the photographic assets already in the repo.

# Interview

1. **Vibe in three to five words, plus references.**
   Neighbourhood, fluorescent, white-coat, unhurried, known. References: a morning waiting-room light, a family GP corridor, the quiet of a compounding bench. Not "sites we like."

2. **The scroll journey, section by section.**
   You arrive at the first pharmacist. You walk the dispensary floor. You reach the counter where two people are already looking up. You meet the whole team. You land back at the pharmacist who will actually help you, with a filled care card in your hand.

3. **The energy curve.**
   Calm at the door. A little pressure on the floor (the appointment you do not want to wait for). Easier at the counter. Held and human on the team. Quiet resolve at the visit.

4. **How should someone feel, and the ONE moment they remember.**
   Stage by stage: recognition, unease, relief, trust, resolve. The one moment: the whole team is suddenly there, and the care card finishes writing itself.

5. **One thing no site they have seen does.**
   A real Ontario-style care card that fills as you walk the shop, then becomes the visit.

6. **How far from premium-minimal.**
   Editorial-documentary, high-key, close to the existing brand green. Not dark luxury. Not playful. Not brutalist.

7. **One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?**
   One unbroken world. The user named scrollworld. The camera stays inside 157 Queen St E.

8. **What assets do they already have?**
   Four real photographs: Carolyn at the bench, the floor at work, two people at the counter, the full team. Logo, NAP, hours, minor-ailment list, specialized services. No footage. No KIE key. Build from their own photos.

# Feeling curve

1. Recognition. Carolyn in the aisle. You know this kind of shop.
2. Unease. The floor is already working. Copy names the appointment you would rather not book.
3. Relief. Two people at the counter, already looking up. Minor ailments, no referral.
4. Trust. The whole team fills the frame. This is the peak. The care card completes.
5. Resolve. Back to Carolyn. The card is the visit: call, hours, the door on Queen Street.

# Peak

The sentence a visitor would say to a friend: "I walked through the shop and the whole team came up, and the card in the corner had already written itself."

Lives in the Team leg. Largest weight on the page. Silence (almost no copy) on the last third of the Counter leg in front of it.

# Tell-someone

It's the site where you walk the Queen Street pharmacy and a care card fills itself as you pass each counter.

# Authored silence

The last third of the Counter leg holds the photograph with no type, so the team arrival has something to arrive from. Verification: that stretch is silence on purpose. The film still advances. There is no copy window on that beat.

# Journey

1. Recognition. They see the pharmacist they would actually meet.
2. Tension. The cost of waiting for a clinic.
3. Turn. A pharmacist can prescribe for the ordinary things.
4. Substance / peak. The team that does the work.
5. Commitment. Call or walk in. One action, one label: Visit us.

# Grammar

Continuous world, worldflight mode.

Why the other seven lost:
- Filmic one-shot is the template this skill exists to refuse, and the brief is a place, not a film argument.
- Chaptered editorial would print a feature. This is a shop you move through.
- Live surface is for software.
- Typographic poster would throw away the only real proof they own: these faces.
- Gallery would catalogue products. They sell care, not a range of objects.
- Split stage needs two sides. There is one counter.
- Rhythmic cutlist is the wrong pulse for a neighbourhood pharmacy.

# Signature move

**The care card.** A paper slip fixed to the page for the whole flight. Each waypoint inks a real line (address, services, 13 minor ailments, owner, hours and phone). At arrival the slip is complete and is the visit. Not a spotlight, not a magnet, not a kit rail.

# Art direction

High-key documentary. Available fluorescent light, white coats, honest blur. Grade: cool bone, muted green, no cinematic crush. Style preamble for any future generation (not used this run):

> Documentary photography, available fluorescent clinic light, handheld 35mm. Natural skin tones, honest imperfect surfaces, slight motion in the frame. High-key, muted realistic grade of cool bone and pharmacy green, visible grain. Candid, unposed. Absolutely not stock-photo styling, no fake smiles, no CGI, NOT clay, NOT illustration.

# 2026 editorial revision

The initial continuous-world build was superseded after visual review. The requested reference is the Wilk & Wilk site, whose strength comes from a varied editorial rhythm rather than a single fixed stage. The live Queen Lynch homepage now uses an **editorial-journey** grammar while preserving the original documentary photography, care-first story, and prescription-label idea.

## Revised journey

1. Sticky full-bleed hero: immediate place, promise, and direct actions.
2. Offset trust split: a real pharmacist portrait beside the neighbourhood-care proposition.
3. Pinned horizontal service rail: four distinct services revealed through movement.
4. Sticky minor-ailment scene: a full-scale prescription label fills as the visitor proceeds.
5. Specialized-care split: portrait, supporting copy, and three practical service rows.
6. Long-form story collage: layered pharmacy photographs move at alternating rates.
7. Circular visit reveal: photography expands behind one clear decision point.

## Revised signature move

**The full-scale care label.** The former fixed corner card became the dominant object in its own sticky scene. A progress rule, grouped minor ailments, and approval stamp resolve with scroll. The treatment is large enough to read, specific to a pharmacy, and remains complete when motion is reduced.
